<SummarySection title="Summary">

Vigía is a progressive web app that unifies the operation of a pharmacy: batch-level inventory with FEFO dispatch (first expired, first out), expiry tracking, pricing, supplier purchasing, point of sale, cash register and reconciliation, and store credit. It also serves customers with a web catalog showing stock and price per branch, and covers health-regulation obligations: the controlled-substance log, product recalls and batch traceability.

It serves two customer profiles: the neighborhood pharmacy, where the same person sells, receives goods and closes the register, and the multi-branch chain, which needs consolidated stock, transfers and central promotions.

</SummarySection>

<ObjectivesSection title="Goals">

The base specification defines five goals:

1. Reduce the time staff spend answering repetitive questions over WhatsApp or phone.
2. Avoid losses from expiry and from stockouts of fast-moving products.
3. Eliminate price differences between shelf, register and catalog.
4. Provide traceability for every unit sold, damaged, returned or lost.
5. Retain customers with recurring purchases: chronic patients, older adults and families.

As design constraints, the specification states that the counter never stops: no network, Redis or external-provider failure prevents selling, charging or closing the register. In addition, all patient and prescription data is treated as sensitive from the moment it is captured, in line with Colombian Law 1581 of 2012.

</ObjectivesSection>

<MotivationSection title="Why the product was built">

The base specification starts from pain points that repeat in neighborhood pharmacies and in chains. Among the most important:

- **Repetitive questions.** Dozens of daily WhatsApp messages or calls asking about stock and prices. The answer was a web catalog with real-time stock and price.
- **Expiry and stockouts.** Expired medicines on the shelf and fast-moving products running out without warning. The answer was batch-level control with FEFO and alerts.
- **Inconsistent prices.** Shelf, register and web do not match. The answer was a single price master per SKU and branch, with history and auditing.
- **Health compliance.** Prescriptions as blurry photos, controlled substances without validation, and manual searches for batches affected by a recall. The answer was pharmacist validation before dispensing, and exportable control logs.
- **Loose sales.** About 40% of SKUs are sold in fractions (box, blister, tablet), which does not fit an inventory by package.
- **Dependence on a single person and high turnover.** The interface had to be simple so anyone on the team could sell without long training.

The context also weighs in: margins are thin, staff turns over, internet connectivity is unstable, and health and data-protection obligations leave no room for improvisation.

</MotivationSection>

<ImplementationSection title="How it was built">

### Architecture

- **Monorepo** with `api/`, `apps/web`, `packages/`, `contracts/`, `supabase/` and `deploy/`.
- **Go backend as a modular monolith** with two executables: the API and a worker for asynchronous tasks. Each module is a vertical slice with hexagonal architecture inside: the domain depends only on ports, and the adapters (HTTP, database, email, WhatsApp) plug in from the outside.
- **A single web app** in Next.js with the App Router, organized by features with atomic design, and four surfaces: a public store per pharmacy, staff access, a dashboard with POS, and the platform console.
- **Multi-tenancy in the database.** Every business table carries `tenant_id`, with forced Row-Level Security and `SET LOCAL` per transaction. The application uses its own roles, never Supabase's `service_role` key, which bypasses RLS.
- **Events with a transactional outbox.** The event is written in the same transaction as the business change and published afterwards to asynq on Redis. That way there is no dual write.
- **Inventory as immutable movements.** Every stock change is a movement per batch, in base unit and per branch. The balance is projected in the same transaction, and the deduction uses `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` in canonical order to apply FEFO without negative stock.
- **Offline POS.** The branch stores a bundle in IndexedDB with products, prices and batches. Sales are queued with a UUID v7 identifier and an `Idempotency-Key`, and synchronize without duplicating. Conflicts are logged for human review, not hidden.
- **External providers behind ports.** Twilio and SendGrid are used through adapters, so a sale never waits on a WhatsApp or email delivery.
- **Private files.** Prescriptions and documents live in private buckets, with signed URLs valid for five minutes.
- **Observability and goals as code.** OpenTelemetry, Grafana and SLOs declared in the repository.

<DecisionsSection title="Why it was done this way">

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| A single PWA instead of a native app | One codebase serves the public store, the staff dashboard and the POS, and it runs in the browser with no installation on each machine. |
| Modular monolith with vertical slices | There are two deployables and a small team. Each use case is isolated in its own directory, and dependency rules are verified in CI. |
| Multi-tenancy with RLS and own roles | Three hundred pharmacies are projected on the same database. Filtering only in the application would be a health-data incident, so the database rejects queries without context. |
| Inventory by immutable movements and FEFO with locking | It allows selling fractions without opening boxes, rebuilding the stock ledger and tracing every unit to the customer for a recall. The cost is contention on heavily sold batches at peak hours. |
| Offline POS with IndexedDB | Internet outages at the store were a high risk. The counter must keep selling for eight hours or five hundred sales without a connection. |
| Integrations always asynchronous | An outage at Twilio, SendGrid or the billing provider cannot prevent charging. |
| Compliance in the same transaction | A controlled substance sold without pharmacist validation, or a recalled batch that keeps being sold, is not fixed with a later report. The control-log entry is written together with the sale. |
| Prisma Client Go with pgx for critical operations | Prisma gives a declarative schema and diff detection in CI. Since that library is archived and does not support interactive transactions, operations that read and decide together (FEFO, sequence numbers, changing an operation type) use pgx directly. |

The data-access decision changed during phase 0. The original ADR-004 set sqlc with goose; ADR-014 revised it to Prisma Client Go and it was logged as a risk, because the project has been archived since July 2025.

</DecisionsSection>

<LimitationsSection title="Limitations and open items">

- **Phase 0 declared.** The README describes the foundations scope. The operation modules already have code, but not all backlog features are complete.
- **Modules without a backend yet.** Purchasing, prescriptions, health compliance, cold chain, multi-branch and privacy have no folder in the backend.
- **Electronic invoicing** is a proposal (CAP-023 and ADR-019), pending the choice of a DIAN-authorized provider.
- **Commercial value of the plans** is still open (PA-10), and the plan model is stored as configurable data, not as constants.
- **Web store cache** is invalidated by time (60 seconds), not by event, so stock can take up to a minute to show.
- **Suspending a pharmacy** prevents its owner from approving support access from the dashboard.
- **Hash-chained audit log** is a proposal (ADR-018) and is not yet implemented.

</LimitationsSection>
