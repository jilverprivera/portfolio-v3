<SummarySection title="Summary">

Vigía is a progressive web app that unifies the operation of a pharmacy: batch-level inventory with FEFO dispatch (first expired, first out), expiry tracking, pricing, supplier purchasing, point of sale, cash register and reconciliation, and store credit. It also serves customers with a web catalog showing stock and price per branch, and covers health-regulation obligations: the controlled-substance log, product recalls and batch traceability.

It serves two customer profiles: the neighborhood pharmacy, where the same person sells, receives goods and closes the register, and the multi-branch chain, which needs consolidated stock, transfers and central promotions.

</SummarySection>

<ChallengeSection title="Challenge & strategy">

### Challenge

The story starts at the counter of a neighborhood pharmacy. Dozens of WhatsApp messages and phone calls a day ask the same thing: do you have this, and how much is it? Meanwhile, medicines expire on the shelf and fast-moving products run out without warning. Shelf, register and web show different prices, prescriptions arrive as blurry photos, controlled substances are sold without validation, and finding the batches affected by a recall means searching by hand.

The context makes each of those problems harder. Margins are thin, staff turns over, internet connectivity is unstable, and health and data-protection obligations leave no room for improvisation. About 40% of SKUs are sold in fractions (a box, a blister, a tablet), which does not fit an inventory counted by package. And in many pharmacies one person does everything, so the interface had to be simple enough for anyone on the team to sell without long training.

### Strategy

The base specification turned those pains into five goals: spend less staff time answering repetitive questions, avoid losses from expiry and stockouts, eliminate price differences between shelf, register and catalog, trace every unit sold, damaged, returned or lost, and retain customers with recurring purchases, such as chronic patients, older adults and families.

Each pain got a direct answer. A web catalog with real-time stock and price replaces the repetitive questions. Batch-level control with FEFO and alerts takes care of expiry. A single price master per SKU and branch, with history and auditing, ends the price mismatch. Pharmacist validation before dispensing and exportable control logs cover compliance.

Two rules were set as constraints from the start. The counter never stops: no network, Redis or external-provider failure may prevent selling, charging or closing the register. And all patient and prescription data is treated as sensitive from the moment it is captured, in line with Colombian Law 1581 of 2012.

</ChallengeSection>

<ImplementationSection title="Implementation & final design">

### Architecture

The repository is a monorepo with `api/`, `apps/web`, `packages/`, `contracts/`, `supabase/` and `deploy/`. The backend is a Go modular monolith with two executables, the API and a worker for asynchronous tasks. Each module is a vertical slice with hexagonal architecture inside: the domain depends only on ports, and the adapters (HTTP, database, email, WhatsApp) plug in from the outside. The frontend is a single Next.js app with the App Router, organized by features with atomic design, and it serves four surfaces: a public store per pharmacy, staff access, a dashboard with the POS, and the platform console.

Many pharmacies share one database, so multi-tenancy is enforced there. Every business table carries `tenant_id`, with forced Row-Level Security and `SET LOCAL` per transaction, and the application uses its own roles, never Supabase's `service_role` key, which bypasses RLS. Events follow a transactional outbox: the event is written in the same transaction as the business change and published afterwards to asynq on Redis, so there is no dual write.

Inventory is the heart of the model. Every stock change is an immutable movement per batch, in base unit and per branch, and the balance is projected in the same transaction. The deduction uses `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` in canonical order to apply FEFO without ever going negative.

Then comes the promise that the counter never stops. The branch keeps a bundle of products, prices and batches in IndexedDB, and sales made offline are queued with a UUID v7 identifier and an `Idempotency-Key`, so they synchronize without duplicating. Conflicts are logged for a person to review, not hidden. External providers sit behind ports: Twilio and SendGrid are used through adapters, so a sale never waits on a WhatsApp or email delivery. Prescriptions and documents live in private buckets with signed URLs valid for five minutes, and OpenTelemetry, Grafana and SLOs are declared as code in the repository.

### Decisions

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| A single PWA instead of a native app | One codebase serves the public store, the staff dashboard and the POS, and it runs in the browser with no installation on each machine. |
| Modular monolith with vertical slices | There are two deployables and a small team. Each use case is isolated in its own directory, and dependency rules are verified in CI. |
| Multi-tenancy with RLS and own roles | Three hundred pharmacies are projected on the same database. Filtering only in the application would be a health-data incident, so the database rejects queries without context. |
| Inventory by immutable movements and FEFO with locking | It allows selling fractions without opening boxes, rebuilding the stock ledger and tracing every unit to the customer for a recall. The cost is contention on heavily sold batches at peak hours. |
| Offline POS with IndexedDB | Internet outages at the store were a high risk. The counter must keep selling for eight hours or five hundred sales without a connection. |
| Integrations always asynchronous | An outage at Twilio, SendGrid or the billing provider cannot prevent charging. |
| Compliance in the same transaction | A controlled substance sold without pharmacist validation, or a recalled batch that keeps being sold, is not fixed with a later report. The control-log entry is written together with the sale. |
| Prisma Client Go with pgx for critical operations | Prisma gives a declarative schema and diff detection in CI. Since that library is archived and does not support interactive transactions, operations that read and decide together (FEFO, sequence numbers, changing an operation type) use pgx directly. |

The data-access decision changed during phase 0. The original ADR-004 set sqlc with goose; ADR-014 revised it to Prisma Client Go and it was logged as a risk, because the project has been archived since July 2025.

<ResultsSection title="Results">

For a pharmacy, the result is the foundation of a single system that replaces WhatsApp answers, loose spreadsheets and paper logs. The operation is in place for users, branches and roles, a public store with stock and price, the dashboard with the point of sale, and an immutable audit log. Modules for catalog, pricing, inventory, counts, sales, cash and customers already have working code.

There is still a lot to build. Purchasing, prescriptions, health compliance, cold chain, multi-branch and privacy are not built yet, electronic invoicing is only a proposal, and the web store can take up to a minute to show a stock change. No pilot results are reported yet.

</ResultsSection>
